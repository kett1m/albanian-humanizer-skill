# Narrative craft without a formula

Use for fiction, scripts, and personal narrative. This is original guidance
informed by the distinction between sentence-level polish and story-level
construction, with conceptual credit to joeinvents/humanizerskill and the
StoryScope research. The research is descriptive, not a universal recipe for
good fiction or a validation of Albanian-specific detection.

## Establish the permitted change

A prose polish preserves events, character identities, chronology, point of
view, motives, and ending. Suggest a structural change separately if it would
help. A structural rewrite may alter arrangement within the user's brief, but
does not automatically authorize new events. Original fiction can invent;
memoir and personal essays cannot invent remembered details or quotations.

## Questions that guide a useful revision

- What does each character want in this scene, and what prevents an easy answer?
  Let speech and action serve those aims. Dialogue need not explain the theme.
- Does the narrator explain a meaning the scene already conveys? Removing that
  explanation may strengthen it. Keep an explicit moral when the genre or user
  asks for one, including fables and instructional stories.
- Does every paragraph use the same emotional cue? A direct emotion, an action,
  silence, or an ordinary observation may fit better than repeated bodily signs.
- Does the final paragraph repeat a resolution already achieved? End at the
  right moment for this story. Happy, reflective, closed, and chronological
  stories remain valid choices.
- Does a concrete detail belong to this character and place? In new fiction,
  choose details with a purpose. In nonfiction, use only supplied or verified
  details. Do not decorate Albanian settings with invented folklore or customs.
- Would a secondary thread, changed viewpoint, or time jump actually improve
  this particular work? Use one only when authorized and useful. Microfiction
  may need none of them.

Keep morally straightforward characters when the brief requires them. Do not
add cruelty, profanity, ambiguity, an unresolved ending, or a second location
just to avoid a pattern. Leave intentional rhythm and repetition in poetry or
spoken storytelling. Do not apply fixed claims about a named model's tendencies.

## Two different scopes

Existing-story polish:

Input: `Mira ia ktheu çelësin. “Faleminderit”, tha ai. Ajo u nis për në shtëpi,
duke ndier një ndjenjë qetësie dhe paqeje brenda vetes.`

Possible edit: `Mira ia ktheu çelësin. “Faleminderit”, tha ai. Ajo u nis për në
shtëpi e qetë.`

The edit retains the return, thanks, departure, and calm. It does not invent a
quarrel or change the ending to make it more interesting.

Original fiction: if the brief calls for unresolved tension, an unreturned key
could support it. That is a new story decision, not a permissible silent change
to the source above.
