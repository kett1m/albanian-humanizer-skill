# CEFR targets and translation

Use CEFR A1–C2 as approximate writing or speaking-script targets in either
language. The Council of Europe's framework describes proficiency through
communicative abilities, not a word-count formula. These editing choices are
this project's practical heuristics, not official Albanian or English grading
rules. Dialects do not have lower proficiency ceilings.

| Target | Practical direction for this skill |
| --- | --- |
| A1 | Very familiar words; short complete statements and simple requests. Make participants and actions explicit. Explain unavoidable terms simply. |
| A2 | Familiar everyday language; short linked ideas, basic reasons and sequences. Reduce idioms and long embedded clauses. |
| B1 | Connected, straightforward prose about familiar matters; clear reasons, experience, and simple conditional relationships. |
| B2 | Clear developed explanations and arguments; useful detail, comparisons, and qualification without unnecessary jargon. |
| C1 | Flexible, precise expression, coherent complex reasoning, appropriate register, and controlled idiom. |
| C2 | Fine distinctions and fluent, economical control of complex material. Precision may require a simple sentence. |

Do not add grammar mistakes at lower levels. Do not make C2 ornate, archaic,
longer by default, or supposedly “more native” than a dialect. Word rarity and
sentence length alone cannot establish a CEFR level. A single short sentence
may be appropriate at several levels.

## Lowering the level

Split nested clauses, make references explicit, use common equivalents, and
explain necessary terminology in context. Preserve conditions, exceptions,
negation, timing, and exact values. A longer explanation may be easier than a
short technical sentence. Do not silently summarize when asked to simplify.

If a complex legal, medical, or technical distinction resists A1/A2, retain it
and say briefly that the version uses mostly simple language but keeps the
necessary term or qualification. Do not assert exact compliance or replace an
operative text with a legally equivalent version. A learner explanation may
accompany an unchanged original when that is the appropriate deliverable.

## Raising the level

Improve precision, cohesion, and control of relationships already in the source.
Combine clauses when useful and select exact vocabulary. Do not introduce new
arguments, sophisticated-sounding evidence, idioms without context, or extra
certainty. A B1 text rewritten at C2 should still make the same claims.

## A small ladder in both languages

All rows retain the office closure on Friday and email access. These are
illustrations of editorial choices, not certified examples or exclusive levels.

| Target | English | Shqipja standarde |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | The office is closed on Friday. You can send us an email. | Zyra është e mbyllur të premten. Mund të na dërgoni një email. |
| A2 | The office is closed on Friday, but you can send us an email. | Zyra është e mbyllur të premten, por mund të na dërgoni një email. |
| B1 | The office is closed on Friday. You can still contact us by email. | Zyra është e mbyllur të premten. Megjithatë, mund të na kontaktoni me email. |
| B2 | Although the office is closed on Friday, you can still contact us by email. | Edhe pse zyra është e mbyllur të premten, mund të na kontaktoni me email. |
| C1 | You can still contact us by email while the office is closed on Friday. | Mund të na kontaktoni me email edhe gjatë mbylljes së zyrës të premten. |
| C2 | The office is closed on Friday; you can still contact us by email. | Zyra është e mbyllur të premten; mund të na kontaktoni me email. |

Do not promise an email response on Friday: none of the source facts establishes
that. C1 and C2 do not require a needlessly elaborate version of this notice.

## Both directions, same meaning

Identify source and target independently. Examples of valid transformations:

- English C2 → English B1, or English B1 → English C2.
- Albanian C1 → Albanian A2, or Albanian B1 → Albanian C1.
- English B2 → standard Albanian B1, or Kosovo Albanian → English C1.
- English C1 → conversational Gheg B2, or Tosk Albanian → English B2.

For a cross-language request, first understand the propositions and pragmatic
intent, then express them naturally in the target, then adjust complexity.
Do not translate each word and repair the result afterwards. Keep honorifics,
requests, irony, and emotional stance proportionate to the source.

Dates, decimals, currency, measurements, ranges, and deadlines retain their
values. Localize their presentation only when unambiguous and requested or
appropriate; do not convert currencies or units by assumption. `03/04/2026`
needs context before it can become a month-name date. Keep timezone information.

Use a small terminology map for recurring technical terms in a long document.
Check that each translated paragraph retains every material proposition and
its corresponding citation. Translation is not fact-checking. Preserve source
uncertainty and flag consequential ambiguity instead of guessing.

## Speech and dialogue

`medium=speech` means a script suitable to say aloud: clear referents, natural
breath groups, and fewer deeply nested clauses. A presentation can remain formal.
`medium=dialogue` also respects turn-taking and the relationship between speakers.
Neither permits invented hesitation, filler, or errors as a proxy for proficiency.
Without audio and an appropriate assessment process, do not evaluate pronunciation,
accent, listening, interaction, or the user's overall CEFR level.
