# English — Anglisht

Preserve an established UK or US convention unless the user chooses another.
For new text without a regional preference, use widely understood vocabulary
and consistent spelling, avoiding unnecessary regional idioms. Do not infer a
US audience just because the language is English.

Prefer natural collocations over literal Albanian syntax. Albanian can omit a
recoverable subject; ordinary English finite clauses generally need one.
Recover the intended subject from context rather than inventing an actor.
Where Albanian leaves gender unspecified, retain that uncertainty with a name,
neutral rephrasing, or singular `they` when appropriate.

Match contractions to voice and purpose: `can't` may suit a message, `cannot`
a formal instruction. Neither is automatically more human. Keep terms of art,
necessary passive voice, genuine hedging, and deliberate repetition. Do not
upgrade every everyday verb into a Latinate synonym for C1 or C2.

Translate `më vjen keq` by function: `I'm sorry` for an apology, or an appropriate
expression of sympathy when responding to bad news. Translate `të lutem` and
`ju lutem` with appropriate politeness rather than using `please` in every clause.
Do not turn a Gheg or Kosovo voice into caricatured English dialect or misspellings.
Represent informality through ordinary English register.

Input: `We would like to take this opportunity to inform you that your request
has been received by our team and will be reviewed within two working days.`

Edit: `We've received your request and will review it within two working days.`

Keep `within two working days`; changing it to `soon` loses the promise. If a
formal house style avoids contractions, use `We have received`.

Input: `S'kam mujt me ardhë dje, po dokumentin ta kam dërgu.`

Translation: `I couldn't come yesterday, but I sent you the document.`

`Couldn't` preserves inability. `Didn't want to` would change the meaning.

For speaking scripts, write phrases that can be spoken comfortably, with clear
referents and sensible pauses. Add pronunciation notes only when requested and
do not claim to measure speaking skill from the script.
