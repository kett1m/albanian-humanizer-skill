# Shared Albanian decisions

Write Albanian as Albanian, not English with substituted nouns. Check the
relationship between verbs, complements, clitics, articles, and noun cases.
Preserve meaning before trying to make a sentence more idiomatic.

- Check gender and number agreement, definite and indefinite noun forms,
  prepositions and case, linking articles, and clitic order. Recast a difficult
  sentence instead of guessing an inflection.
- Albanian often leaves a recoverable subject pronoun unexpressed. Do not repeat
  `unë`, `ne`, or `ju` merely because the English source repeats its subject.
  Keep a pronoun when it marks emphasis, contrast, or prevents ambiguity.
- Preserve person, tense, aspect, modality, negation, and politeness. `ti` and
  `ju` are not interchangeable; `ju` may be plural or polite singular. Do not
  infer gender or familiarity without support.
- Prefer a natural verb to a stack of abstract nouns: `bëjmë vlerësimin e
  kërkesave` can become `vlerësojmë kërkesat`. Retain institutional terminology
  when it has a defined meaning.
- Use `ë` and `ç` in standard writing. For dialect, follow the selected written
  convention or sample. Restoring letters is contextual, not a global `e → ë`
  or `c → ç` substitution. `te` and `të` have different functions.
- `orthography=ascii` is an explicit delivery constraint: render Albanian prose
  `ë/Ë → e/E` and `ç/Ç → c/C`, and use plain equivalents for dialect diacritics.
  Do not change protected names, identifiers, quoted originals, or links without
  permission. Do not equate ASCII spelling with Kosovo or Gheg identity.
- Use ordinary sentence case in headings, subject to proper names and house
  style. Do not import English title capitalization or hyphenation mechanically.
- Preserve names of people and organizations as supplied. Use established local
  place names when translation calls for them, without renaming brands or making
  assumptions about citizenship, ethnicity, or political preference.

## Translation traps

Translate the function of an expression. “I look forward to hearing from you”
in a business letter may be `Pres përgjigjen tuaj`; it need not become an
overwrought declaration of excitement. “Apply” may mean `aplikoj`, `paraqes një
kërkesë`, or `zbatoj`; “address” may be an address, an audience, or an issue.
Choose from context. Keep useful loanwords in technical or local usage instead
of performing language purism.

`Eventualisht` should not be selected as a word-for-word rendering of English
“eventually”; express the actual intended timing, such as `në fund` when that is
what the source means. Translate “actually” by its function, often `në fakt`,
instead of assuming it means `aktualisht` (“currently”).

Do not interpret every mixed English term as an error. A team's `deploy`,
`pull request`, or product label may be intentional. Preserve the user's glossary.
When a term is unfamiliar at the requested level, explain it briefly at first use
without replacing the technical meaning.

## Register and regional boundaries

Formality, dialect, proficiency, and medium are separate choices. A Gheg speaker
can write at C2. A formal text for Kosovo can use standard grammar. A low-level
learner text should still sound respectful and grammatically complete.

Do not mix Shkodër literary conventions, contemporary Prishtina colloquialisms,
and southern regional forms simply to increase dialect visibility. Some shared
forms are legitimate; coherence matters more than maximizing differences.
Preserve a speaker's consistent spelling unless normalization is requested.

For a local rendering beyond your confidence, ask for a short sample or state
that the rendering is broad. Do not invent phonetic spellings or call them an
official dialect standard. Read the selected profile for concrete choices.
